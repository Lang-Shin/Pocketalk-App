# Semantic action evidence experiment

This isolated experiment examines synthetic responses; it does not execute transactions or activate an online provider. An accepted result means only that the experimental checks passed, not that a transaction is safe or authorized.

The module is not imported by production assistant selection. Its candidate data is not an `AssistantResult` and cannot itself pass assistant-result validation as a proposal. Before reporting experimental acceptance, the evaluator constructs a temporary validation-only result and checks it with the existing assistant validator, including current source balances and routing. This is not a ledger transaction or an execution authorization. It has no provider, network, database, finance execution, confirmation, or credential access. Any future integration must revalidate current state and require confirmation; the experimental outcome alone is insufficient.

Experimental acceptance additionally requires an injected trusted eligibility snapshot dated to the reference local date, matching nonempty synthetic snapshot/current revision tokens, and wallet lifecycle statuses for referenced wallets. Missing, stale, malformed, or incomplete evidence requests clarification; an explicitly archived wallet is rejected. The production wallet type currently has no lifecycle status, so this synthetic status is an experimental input, not a claim that production supplies it. Balances are taken solely from local context, not candidate fields. A same-day date alone does not establish freshness. Matching test tokens demonstrate a test policy only: they do not prove that the caller has authoritative revisions or prevent changes after evaluation. A future integration would still require trusted state and execution-time validation.

Semantic evidence concerns the message, action, amount, date and entity roles. Snapshot eligibility evidence concerns the injected balances, statuses and test revision tokens. Authoritative execution-time eligibility belongs to the existing deterministic finance flow and explicit confirmation; this experiment does not access that flow or live finance data.

Explicitly negated action statements are rejected; ambiguous occurrence, questions, hypotheses and unsupported evidence request clarification. Financial ineligibility and malformed fields are rejected; a well-formed unsupported action is marked unsupported. These outcomes describe the experiment and do not authorize transactions.

The response intent must match its action. Action evidence comes from explicit phrases listed in `ACTION_EVIDENCE` in `src/ai/online/semanticEvaluation.ts`, including expense, income, transfer and Savings terms. The bounded implicit examples include “set me back” for expense and “set aside” for a Savings contribution, which additionally requires a Savings mention. Unsupported action wording and detected negation or hypothetical wording require clarification. Income wording combined with transfer-like wording, including “padala”, requires clarification. Detected independent action categories or repeated action phrases are rejected rather than collapsed into one transaction. The offline baseline is recorded separately; an existing offline proposal is returned as unsupported by this experiment rather than replaced.

These rules are deliberately conservative and can reject harmless wording. Keyword matching alone cannot resolve intent, negation, quoted instructions, endpoint direction, all multi-action statements, or other semantic ambiguity. The experiment does not establish model accuracy, validate all financial eligibility, or replace deterministic validation and explicit confirmation. Further adversarial evaluation is required before considering integration.

The non-transaction screen requests clarification for bounded negation directly attached to an action, questions, hypothetical wording, cancellation instructions, and quoted text. Negation of an unrelated phrase alone does not trigger that screen. It intentionally errs on the side of clarification and is not a general-purpose negation or quotation parser.

## Amount evidence

The candidate amount is parsed using the existing peso parser and must be a positive safe integer number of centavos. The experiment requires exactly one numeric token outside ISO dates, directly after a supported action phrase. It rejects detected arithmetic, signs, times, malformed formatting, and additional numeric tokens. A restricted set of words can follow the amount, preventing common counts from being treated as pesos. These restrictions intentionally reject some legitimate expressions and are not a complete natural-language quantity classifier; ambiguous or unsupported forms need further evaluation.

## Date evidence

The reference date is injected as `context.todayDateKey`; the evaluator does not read the device clock. A single explicit ISO date or supported today/yesterday expression is resolved against that reference, and must be valid, not future-dated, and in the active month. With no detected date, the experiment uses the existing current-local-date default. Conflicting detected expressions, written months, unsupported relative periods, and slash-form dates are rejected. This restricted recognition does not cover all natural-language date expressions; it is not evidence that unrecognized phrases are safe.

## Entity evidence

References are resolved through the request's local wallet and active-container aliases, never from model-provided persistent IDs. The experiment requires explicit unique entity names, rejects conflicting or irrelevant alias fields, and asks for clarification when additional entities are mentioned. Transfers additionally require a recognized `from <source> to/for <destination>` route. This is intentionally narrower than the application's defaults and natural-language coverage; ambiguous or unsupported wording is not used to select an entity.

## Read-only and unsupported results

The result retains the offline interpretation, including locally calculated answers. A local read-only answer makes the synthetic candidate unsupported; no candidate-generated totals or balances are returned. A synthetic clarification's missing fields are returned only when the local clarification independently agrees on intent and every field, its reason is compatible, and bounded message checks do not contradict it. Otherwise the result is unsupported without model-selected fields. Unknown candidate entity aliases use a distinct unsupported reason; missing destination claims require a locally named source and no explicitly named destination. These deliberately narrow checks do not resolve every unknown natural-language entity or every ambiguous amount. Proposal-declared missing fields are also treated as unsupported. A refusal or a synthetic answer unsupported by local interpretation remains unsupported. The experiment does not create a financial answer from model output.

## Synthetic benchmark scoring

The benchmark retains the original 24 fixtures and adds adversarial cases. Independent expected proposal labels specify intent, action, integer amount, date, and applicable wallet/container roles; expected decisions also specify an outcome and reason. Non-proposal candidate labels independently specify kind, intent, topic, missing fields, and/or refusal reason. The scorer validates candidate contracts and compares non-proposals exactly, so unsupported extra fields or wrong topics and reasons do not count as correct.

The benchmark separately counts fully matching offline proposals, correct offline non-proposals for three explicitly labeled cases, correctly formed synthetic proposals, correct synthetic non-proposals for six explicitly labeled cases, matching experimental decisions, unsafe acceptances, and accepted full-field matches beyond an offline proposal. These subset counts are not overall assistant accuracy scores. The experiment now withholds unsupported clarification fields; when local rules cannot establish a needed field, even a correct candidate field is unsupported and is scored as incomplete. A refusal's specific reason is scored at the candidate level; the evaluator's generic unsupported status does not expose that reason. The candidates are manually constructed and do not measure model accuracy. An archived-container fixture observes that aliases are rebuilt after filtering, so its rejected `c1` resolves to Savings rather than representing a persistent stale alias.

An additional test-only ground-truth scorer (`tests/helpers/semanticBenchmark.ts`) separates semantic classification from financial-field correctness and labels occurrence, eligibility and safe acceptance independently. The benchmark includes an eight-case pilot (`actual-expense`, `valid-offline-command`, `needs-amount`, `wrong-question`, `local-report`, `unsupported-topic`, `refusal`, `negated`) and the first 10 legacy cases in their existing source order (`explicit-expense`, `implicit-expense`, `implicit-expense-cents`, `explicit-income`, `implicit-income`, `wallet-transfer`, `container-transfer`, `savings-contribution`, `savings-withdrawal`, `relative-date`), totaling 18 independently scored cases. The 8 pilot cases remain unchanged, and the remaining 29 legacy cases remain available in the legacy fixture suite for later migration; no original legacy case has been deleted.

The 29 remaining legacy IDs are: `missing-wallet`, `missing-container`, `read-only`, `unsupported`, `clarification`, `ambiguous-padala`, `multiple-actions`, `invented-amount`, `invented-date`, `invented-wallet`, `injection`, `arithmetic`, `extra-field`, `action-substitution`, `negated-expense`, `insufficient-wallet`, `negation-unrelated`, `hypothetical`, `question`, `quoted`, `cancelled`, `exact-balance`, `stale-balance`, `archived-wallet`, `archived-container`, `reversed-wallets`, `wrong-answer-topic`, `wrong-clarification`, and `wrong-refusal`.

### Ground-truth schema and labeling

`SemanticGroundTruth` explicitly records an identifier, message, semantic outcome, intent, complete expected proposal or null, clarification fields and reason, refusal classification, expected read-only topic and figures, transaction occurrence/request, eligibility, and whether acceptance is safe. Labels are authored independently of the evaluator and candidate. Proposal labels hold integer minor units, effective date, and applicable source and destination IDs; absent roles are not applicable. The scorer treats the experimental result and candidate as observations, not as ground truth.

All 10 migrated legacy cases use this independent schema, preserving original fixture messages and synthetic candidate payloads. In legacy position 7 (`container-transfer`: "Move 120 from Food & drinks to Unallocated"), the old fixture marked the transfer unsafe and expected rejection. That label conflated the experimental assistant's restricted ordinary-transfer route with the finance-domain return operation: `PROJECT_SPEC.md` §7.9 explicitly permits returning funds from an ordinary container to Unallocated, and the fixture source holds sufficient funds. The independent label therefore describes a financially eligible container transfer. The experimental evaluator still rejects it as `ordinary_transfer_targets_system_container`; the shared score records this as a false rejection. This documents a mismatch with the broader finance workflow, not permission to change production assistant behavior.

### Scoring and measured categories

The independently scored benchmark contains 18 labeled cases: 12 proposals, 2 clarifications, 1 read-only answer, 1 unsupported request, 1 refusal, and 1 rejected non-transaction. Classification and exact financial fields are scored separately. Clarifications check missing fields and reason; read-only cases check the local topic and figures against labeled local data. The benchmark tests score a valid command as a classification error if it is marked unsupported. Unsupported or refusal outcomes do not automatically count as successful proposals.

`summarizeTriplets` reports counts with denominators: classifications over all labeled cases; false clarifications, false rejections, unsupported classification errors and additional coverage over labeled proposal cases; unsafe and incorrect acceptances over accepted cases; and field mismatches over observable fields. A false acceptance is an accepted candidate that is not labeled safe, lacks eligibility, or fails exact matching. A false rejection is a labeled proposal returned as rejected. An unsupported result on a labeled proposal is separately counted as an unsupported classification error; a clarification on a labeled proposal is separately counted as a false clarification. Neither is claimed as successful interpretation.

Every migrated legacy case runs through the shared triplet scorer (`scoreBenchmarkTriplet`); the migration introduces no second scoring implementation. The older 39-case benchmark retains all original fixtures and reports subset proposal and non-proposal counts using its legacy schema; the other 29 legacy cases remain available for subsequent migration. Do not expand the benchmark to 60 yet.

The test-only `scoreBenchmarkTriplet` applies a shared component comparison to offline, synthetic candidate, and experimental observations across all 18 benchmark cases. `summarizeTriplets` reports classification and exact matches separately for each path, plus proposal safety, conservative outcomes, and per-field mismatches with explicit denominators. A field that cannot be observed is not applicable to that field's mismatch denominator; a classification mismatch still prevents an exact match.

The current 18-case triplet run (8 pilot + 10 migrated legacy in source order) reports offline classification 7/18 and exact 6/18, synthetic candidate classification 16/18 and exact 13/18, and experimental classification 9/18 and exact 8/18. It reports 6 safe additional proposals out of 12 labeled proposal cases, zero unsafe acceptances out of 6 accepted cases, zero incorrect acceptances out of 6 accepted cases, zero false clarifications and one false rejection out of 12 proposal cases, and 5 unsupported classification errors out of 12 proposal cases. The false rejection is the return-to-Unallocated case described above. These are measurements of the hand-labeled synthetic benchmark, not results for an LLM.

### Metamorphic checks

Tests specify expected effects for punctuation, negation, amount changes, wallet changes, a second transaction, balance changes and transfer-direction reversal. They assert specific fields or non-acceptance rather than assuming every transformation preserves meaning. These tests are separate from the labeled pilot counts and do not establish general language coverage.

### Limitations and future hypotheses

The observed results concern hand-written synthetic candidates, not output from a language model. They do not measure a future LLM's accuracy, performance, latency, or safety. Clarification grounding is currently limited to what the offline interpreter independently establishes; pattern-based language grounding and prompt-injection handling remain incomplete. Synthetic wallet status and revision tokens are not production lifecycle or authoritative execution-time eligibility data. Experimental acceptance is never authorization and does not replace production validation, confirmation, or transaction-time balance checks. No live data source, model, provider, or network is connected to this experiment.
